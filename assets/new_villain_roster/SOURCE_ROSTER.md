# HOTEL FOR SUPERVILLAINS — THE GRAND DISASTER

# New guest roster: 30 original concepts

**Concept design only.** No 3D models, Blender work, or Blender scripts. The earlier named roster is not carried forward. Every mission is a proposed playable hotel task, not a claim that it exists in a build.

## CHARACTER 01 — QUEUEJACK

**NUMBER:** 1
**NAME:** Queuejack
**ONE-SENTENCE HOOK:** A line-cutting villain who can make any guest believe he was here first.
**POWER / GIMMICK:** Briefly swaps two people's perceived place in a queue; no visible particles.
**PERSONALITY:** Smooth, impatient, relentlessly certain.
**COMEDY:** Claims to have been waiting for hours while visibly entering the lobby.
**BODY TYPE:** Very tall, narrow, forward-leaning humanoid.
**HEAD / FACE:** Long head, tiny smug eyes, apologetic-looking smile that means nothing.
**SIMPLE OUTFIT:** Single striped overcoat over plain pants.
**SIGNATURE ACCESSORY:** One collapsible queue-post baton held in hand.
**COLOR PALETTE:** Ink blue, cream, brass.
**SILHOUETTE:** Question-mark lean and one long vertical baton.
**HOTEL PROBLEM:** The check-in line reorganizes itself around him and two guests both claim the front spot.
**HOTEL REQUEST:** 'Please confirm I was first without asking anyone else.'
**GAMEPLAY MISSION:** Compare the guests' numbered claim tickets, then physically reorder the lobby queue markers.
**MODELING DIFFICULTY:** Easy
**WHY PLAYERS WOULD REMEMBER THEM:** The very tall guest who cheats at waiting.

# Roster review

Reviewed names, hooks, silhouettes, powers, hotel problems, requests, and playable missions for repetition. The earlier draft had two screen-rotation missions and two fabric/color-matching missions: Rubberneck now needs privacy film; Patchwork Pete now needs a door-latch repair; the similar luxury décor critic was replaced entirely by Airmail. Tomorrow’s request now concerns plumbing rather than another linen mission.

All entries use one accessory and 2–4 colors; no character requires a cluster of floating parts. The silhouettes range from a thin queue-cutting figure to a bottle creature, a drape, a folded-paper flyer, a flat slab, a crate, and a small robot. Villains without supernatural powers are present alongside guests whose powers inconvenience hotel service. Missions vary across queue ordering, missing-item searches, dialogue, maintenance, delivery, cleaning, escorting, seating, signage, and lighting.

**Difficulty mix:** Easy: 12, Easy-Medium: 15, Medium: 3.
